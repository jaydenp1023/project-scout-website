New York procurement guide

How to find New York government contracts

New York posts public procurement opportunities for construction, professional services, cleaning, grounds maintenance and more. The best approach is to search the official state source, then check each buyer's actual requirements.

Where are New York government bids posted?

New York State Contract Reporter (NYSCR) is an important place to begin. The NYS Contract Reporter describes itself as New York's official state procurement publication and offers free registration and bid notifications. It carries notices from state entities and also many other participating public buyers; it should not be treated as a complete listing of every municipal bid.

Don't confuse an opportunity listing with approval to bid. Read the full documents for deadlines, registration, insurance, licensing, certifications and bid instructions. A site that helps you discover a notice cannot determine those conditions for you.

Build a New York bid-search routine

  1. Register with the NYS Contract Reporter. The official service offers a free account, search access and bid notifications; use trade keywords and relevant categories to find opportunities.
  2. Look beyond statewide listings. Cities, counties, school districts and public authorities may publish procurement information on their own portals. Check them if you serve a specific region.
  3. Check federal solicitations too. New York-based companies may pursue federal opportunities through SAM.gov, with different registration and compliance steps.

Example search: janitorial and landscaping

A cleaning company might search for janitorial, custodial services and floor care. A landscaping business might look for grounds maintenance and snow removal. Pay attention to service area and whether a contract covers one building or several sites.

New York-specific checks before bidding

  • Check the notice's response deadline, contracting entity, questions period and any revisions or addenda.
  • Review the vendor-responsibility and disclosure requirements listed by the issuing buyer; some state procurements use a Vendor Responsibility Questionnaire.
  • Confirm applicable insurance, bonding, licensing, wage and workforce requirements for the project.
  • Review winter weather, travel and multi-site service demands when calculating your costs.

One last thing to verify

Registering for alerts helps you discover notices, but it does not mean your business is qualified for any particular contract. The current solicitation documents govern eligibility and responses.

This is an educational search guide, not a live inventory of contracts, an eligibility determination, or legal/procurement advice. Official public sources remain available to search independently.

Quick bid checklist

Before spending time on a response, confirm:

  • Worksite and travel
  • Trade and scope
  • Deadline and amendments
  • Mandatory registration
  • Licenses and insurance
  • Bonding and capacity
